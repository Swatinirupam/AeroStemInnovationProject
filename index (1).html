AeroSTEM Innovation Challenge

Clean Power Flight: designing cleaner ways to power the aircraft of the future

Linked to SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

Our whole-school challenge

Aeroplanes help us travel and connect, but most planes today burn fuel that pollutes the air we all breathe. Across the school we are asking one big question: how might we power flight in cleaner ways, so the air stays healthy for everyone?

Every year group works on this together at their own level. You will follow the same design journey that real engineers use: Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test, Iterate, and then Showcase your idea.

KS1 (Years 1 to 2)

Your project

You are a plane designer. Design a Clean Sky Plane that flies without making dirty smoke.

Look and Think

Some planes make dirty smoke that is not good for the air we breathe. Think about how a plane could be clean instead.

  • Look at pictures of planes and talk about clean air and dirty air.
  • Say who is helped when the air is clean (all of us, animals and plants).

You have finished this step when you can say why clean air matters.

Whole-class talk with picture prompts. Partner stem: Clean air helps...

Dream Up Ideas

Think of lots of ways a plane could fly clean. No idea is too silly!

  • Draw or say as many clean plane ideas as you can (sun power, wind, a kite plane).
  • Pick your favourite idea to make.

You have finished this step when you have chosen one idea to make.

Celebrate quantity. Scribe ideas for children who prefer to talk.

Make It

Make your Clean Sky Plane. You can use a drawing or junk materials.

  • Make a model or a colourful labelled drawing of your clean plane.
  • Point to the part that makes it clean.

You have finished this step when you have made your plane.

Provide junk materials. Keep it playful and quick.

Try It

Show your plane and see what people think.

  • Show a friend and ask: does it look clean? do you like it?
  • Give your friend a happy comment about their plane.

You have finished this step when a friend has seen your plane.

Model kind comments: I like...

Make It Better

Change one thing to make your plane even better.

  • Add or change one thing on your plane.
  • Say what you made better.

You have finished this step when you have made one thing better.

One change only. Ask the child to say the improvement out loud.

Show It

Show your Clean Sky Plane to the class and say who it helps.

  • Show your plane in a show and tell.
  • Say: my clean plane helps keep the air clean.

You have finished this step when you have shown your plane to the class.

Invite a buddy class as the audience.

Lower KS2 (Years 3 to 4)

Your project

You are a young engineer. Design and make a glider that flies with no engine and no fuel at all, so it makes no pollution.

Define the problem

Engines that burn fuel pollute the air. A glider flies with no engine at all. Work out what makes a glider fly well.

  • Talk about why a glider with no fuel is kinder to the air.
  • Agree three things your glider must do, for example fly straight and land safely (your criteria).

You have finished this step when you have three clear criteria.

Co-build criteria. Sentence stem: A good glider will...

Ideate

Think of different glider shapes and wings before you choose one.

  • Sketch at least three glider designs with different wings.
  • Choose one design and say why.

You have finished this step when you have chosen one design and can say why.

Encourage varied wing shapes. Quick vote against the criteria.

Prototype

Make your glider from paper or light junk materials. Rough is fine.

  • Build your glider.
  • Label the wings and the nose.

You have finished this step when you have a glider you can test.

Paper, straws and light card. Keep spares ready.

Design your prototype in Canva

Open Canva, start a new design (a poster works well) or use Magic Media, then add your own labels.

Prompt starter"A simple, colourful labelled diagram of a paper glider with big wings for a school science poster."

Test

Fly your glider and see how it does against your criteria.

  • Fly it three times and record how far and how straight it goes.
  • Ask a partner for one thing that works and one to improve.

You have finished this step when you have flown it more than once and have results.

Fair test talk: launch it the same way each time.

Iterate

Use what you saw to make it fly better.

  • Make two changes, for example fold the wings or add a small weight.
  • Say what changed and whether it helped.

You have finished this step when your glider meets more of your criteria.

Compare before and after distances to show the point of iterating.

Enterprise Showcase

Share your glider and explain why no fuel means cleaner air.

  • Show your glider in a class poster or a short talk.
  • Explain how it links to SDG 7.

You have finished this step when you have shared it and linked it to the SDG.

Poster template: problem, idea, who it helps.

Upper KS2 (Years 5 to 6)

Your project

You are an aircraft engineer. Design a small aircraft powered by clean energy, such as batteries charged by the sun, and show how it gets its power.

Define the problem

Burning fuel pollutes. Clean energy like batteries or solar power can drive small aircraft. Work out what your clean aircraft must do.

  • Discuss clean energy sources for flight (battery, solar) and who benefits.
  • Write a clear problem and agree three criteria.

You have finished this step when you can say who you help and you have three criteria.

Introduce energy sources simply. Co-build the criteria.

Ideate

Think of many designs and clean power ideas, then choose one.

  • Sketch at least five ideas showing how each is powered cleanly.
  • Choose one and explain how it meets your criteria.

You have finished this step when you have chosen one idea and can justify it.

Use a decision grid against the criteria.

Prototype

Build a model that shows your aircraft and where its clean power comes from.

  • Make a cardboard or Lego model, or a labelled diagram.
  • Show clearly where the battery or solar panel goes.

You have finished this step when you have a model or labelled design to test.

Keep the power system visible and central.

Design your prototype in Canva

Open Canva, start a new design (a poster works well) or use Magic Media, then add your own labels.

Prompt starter"A labelled diagram of a small aircraft powered by solar panels and batteries, clean and modern, for a school poster."

Test

Check your design against your criteria and gather feedback.

  • Score your design against each criterion.
  • Get peer feedback: one strength and one improvement.

You have finished this step when you have results for all criteria and some feedback.

Use a kind, specific critique protocol.

Iterate

Improve your design using what you learned.

  • Make at least two improvements and record why.
  • Explain how the changes help you meet your criteria.

You have finished this step when your design meets more criteria than before.

Capture before and after to value the testing.

Enterprise Showcase

Pitch your clean aircraft and explain the difference it makes.

  • Give a group presentation or poster.
  • Explain how it supports SDG 7 and who it helps.

You have finished this step when you have presented and linked to the SDG.

Invite an audience, a STEM ambassador if possible.

KS3 (Years 7 to 9)

Your project

Design and model an electric or solar-assisted drone for a real job (for example crop monitoring or medical delivery), and reason about the energy trade-offs of powering it cleanly.

Define

Drones can do useful jobs, but how they are powered matters for the environment. Define a real job and the requirements for a cleanly powered drone.

  • Research one useful drone job and its needs (range, payload, flight time).
  • List requirements and constraints, including a clean energy source.

You have finished this step when you have clear requirements and constraints.

Link to real aerospace roles and check sources.

Ideate

Generate concepts, then screen them against your requirements.

  • Use a mind map or SCAMPER to generate concepts.
  • Select one using a simple decision grid.

You have finished this step when you have a justified chosen concept.

Weighted criteria introduce trade-offs early.

Prototype

Model your drone using CAD, Tinkercad or a coded simulation, and keep a build log.

  • Create a CAD or coded model of the drone and its power system.
  • Record design decisions in a build log.

You have finished this step when you have a model and a build log.

Tinkercad or block/text code depending on your kit.

Design your prototype in Canva

Open Canva, start a new design (a poster works well) or use Magic Media, then add your own labels.

Prompt starter"A labelled technical diagram of an electric delivery drone with solar-assisted charging, showing the battery and rotors."

Test

Test fairly, collect data and compare options.

  • Run fair tests (for example glide, lift or energy use) and record data.
  • Compare at least two options in a table.

You have finished this step when you have data comparing options.

Emphasise controlled variables.

Iterate

Iterate from the data and show before and after.

  • Make evidence-led changes and re-test.
  • Show before and after results and explain them.

You have finished this step when your data shows improvement.

The iteration log is evidence of the process.

Enterprise Showcase

Pitch your drone with a clear rationale linked to SDG 7.

  • Deliver a pitch with your rationale and data.
  • Explain the clean energy trade-offs you accepted.

You have finished this step when you have pitched with evidence and an SDG link.

Careers and enterprise framing, with a short Q&A.

KS4 (Years 10 to 11)

Your project

Design a costed concept to power a short regional aircraft route with cleaner energy (battery, hydrogen or sustainable fuel). Justify your energy choice with evidence and evaluate feasibility and impact.

Define

Short regional routes are a realistic first target for cleaner aviation. Define the problem precisely with measurable requirements.

  • Research the route type and its energy demands.
  • Write measurable requirements and success criteria.

You have finished this step when you have measurable requirements and criteria.

Link to GCSE specifications and precise problem framing.

Ideate

Generate and screen concepts against weighted criteria, then justify your choice.

  • Generate several clean-power concepts (battery, hydrogen, sustainable fuel).
  • Screen against weighted criteria and justify the chosen concept.

You have finished this step when you have a justified concept from a weighted screen.

Use a decision matrix and evidence-based justification.

Prototype

Produce a CAD, coded or costed model with notes on materials and choices.

  • Model the concept (CAD or coded) and outline the power system.
  • Note key materials, choices and an indicative cost.

You have finished this step when you have a model with costed choices.

Bring in feasibility and cost thinking from the outset.

Design your prototype in Canva

Open Canva, start a new design (a poster works well) or use Magic Media, then add your own labels.

Prompt starter"A clean, modern infographic comparing battery, hydrogen and sustainable fuel to power a short-haul regional aircraft."

Test

Run controlled quantitative tests, analyse the results and note limitations.

  • Test key variables quantitatively and record data.
  • Analyse the results and state the limitations honestly.

You have finished this step when you have analysed quantitative results.

Data handling, uncertainty and limitations.

Iterate

Optimise, justify each change with evidence, and reflect on trade-offs.

  • Optimise the design and justify each change with data.
  • Reflect on the trade-offs you accepted (cost, range, emissions).

You have finished this step when your design is optimised with justified changes.

Trade-off analysis is the key skill here.

Enterprise Showcase

Deliver a costed enterprise pitch with evidence, feasibility and impact.

  • Present a costed pitch with feasibility and impact.
  • Quantify the emissions saving and link it to SDG 7 and SDG 13.

You have finished this step when you have delivered a costed, evidenced pitch.

Authentic audience with enterprise and pitching skills.
